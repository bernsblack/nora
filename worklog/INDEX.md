| 2026-08-02 | [on-device-speech](./2026-08-02-on-device-speech/) | planned | Review of an external spec proposing `whisper.cpp` and `piper` for offline Afrikaans STT and TTS, compiled natively for iOS and Android. Competent, aimed at the right risk, **and not adoptable as written.** Three substantive objections. It reverses PROJECT.md section 9, which puts the room device on a PWA in Android kiosk mode and lists "native app first" under what was rejected because nothing is validated yet; that is a sequencing error rather than a technical one. **Whisper hallucinates fluent text on silence**, and this device is an always-on microphone in a mostly silent bedroom, which combined with the matcher's containment defect manufactures exactly the voice-with-no-source failure the brief treats as the one that is not small. And its Afrikaans accuracy claim cites nothing, on a population PROJECT.md section 8 already warned that published benchmarks do not describe, being 84 with dysarthria rather than young and clear. Also: it never addresses that both languages are live at once and speakers switch mid-sentence, which Whisper structurally cannot do; it gives no latency figure against `LIGHT_BEFORE_SOUND_MS`; and it does not produce the hardware cost number that section 5's open question explicitly asks for and says not to decide by default. **The finding with the longest reach is that it would have silently removed the privacy enforcement**: `privacy.test.ts` is a source scan over TypeScript, three documents describe it as making the floors literally true at code level, and a native module is invisible to it. What the spec gets right, and it matters, is answering section 5's open question in the defensible direction, transcribe on device and send text rather than audio. Converted from an implementation plan into an evaluation task whose deciding measurement is hallucination rate on room silence, not word error rate. **On 2026-08-03 this task also absorbed what does the matching**, because the matcher work hit a wall that is not a tuning problem: classifying wandering, code-switched speech from an elderly person with dysarthria is what models are good at and token overlap is bad at. The boundary drawn, and it is the load bearing part: **the matcher may become a model, the answer policy may not.** Classification is not generation, so a model may decide the intent and the subject and then hands off to the scripted policy unchanged, because a prompt instruction is a probabilistic constraint on output nobody can prove and this is the path that decides what a woman believes about whether her husband is alive. Two things a model does not fix and must not be sold as fixing: a cloud call cannot be mode one, and silence is the wrong shape for a language model, so the ability to abstain is the first thing to measure and accuracy is the second. **Mastra is back on**, because section 9 deferred it on the condition that we would need an eval harness and that condition is now met; `personas/` is one that grew by hand. Note the cost nobody should discover later: swapping the matcher turns 49 crisp pass and fail scenarios into a scored eval, which is the strongest argument for building the harness before the swap rather than after. **Blocked on a human for the native versus PWA decision, and for whether replacing the matcher is worth it at all** |
