# English

## Register

Plain, concrete, and short. Aim below a B1 reading level for anything the resident hears, which mostly falls out of the two-sentence limit.

- **Present tense.** "Anna is coming at 3", not "Anna will be arriving at 3".
- **Active voice, and the person is the subject** where it is natural. "You are at Willowbrook", not "Willowbrook is where you are located".
- **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence has a comma joining two clauses, check whether it should be two sentences or whether the second half should go.
- **No hedging.** "Lunch at 12", not "Lunch should be at around 12". If we are not confident, we say nothing at all rather than saying it vaguely.
- **Contractions are fine in the family app, sparing on the room screen.** "It is Tuesday morning" reads calmer than "It's Tuesday morning" and is fractionally easier to parse aloud.

## Numerals

- **Times in the answer are digits, without meridiem.** "Lunch at 12", "Anna is coming at 3". `spokenClock` deliberately omits am and pm: a person in this state does not disambiguate on that, and the part of the day is already on the screen.
- **Room numbers are digits.** "room 12".
- **Never a date.** No "14 August", no "the 14th". The day in words is the whole vocabulary for time on this screen.

## Casing

Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings. Title Case reads as a brand and this product is not selling to the person in the room.

Day names and part of day are capitalised only at the start of a sentence: "It is Tuesday morning."

## The banlist

Any of these in a string is a rewrite, not a discussion.

**Marketing AI tells:** unlock, empower, elevate, seamless, effortless, transform, revolutionise, journey, "say goodbye to", "peace of mind", "at your fingertips", "we've got you covered", "designed with love", "it's not X, it's Y", benefit triplets ("simple, safe, and secure").

**Care-sector clichés:** "loved one" (say mother, father, or the person's name), "care journey", "quality time", "golden years", "living well with dementia" as a slogan, "person-centred" as a claim about our own product.

**Service language on the room screen:** "How can I help you?", "Is there anything else?", "Let me know if...", "I'm sorry, I didn't catch that", "Please try again".

**Anything that implies memory:** "remember", "recall", "forget", "as you know", "again", "still". Some of these are unavoidable in family app copy about the illness; none of them belongs in a resident-facing string.

## Checklist

1. Two sentences or fewer, thirty words or fewer, on anything spoken.
2. Present tense, active, one idea per sentence.
3. No banlist term.
4. No exclamation mark in any resident-facing string, and at most one anywhere in the family app.
5. Sentence case.
6. No em dash, no emoji, straight quotes.
7. Read it aloud. If it sounds like a receptionist or a brand, rewrite it.
